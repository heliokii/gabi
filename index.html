The lake keeps the moon

A bangka rocks at its mooring. Its one lantern lays a gold line across the water, and the mist sits low enough to touch.

02Hagdan, the stairᜑᜄ᜔ᜇᜈ᜔

Thirty-six steps, a candle on every third

The stair climbs the old sea wall into town. People light a candle on their way up, and the fireflies keep the ones that went out company.

03Bahay na bato, the stone housesᜊᜑᜌ᜔ ᜈ ᜊᜆᜓ

Stone below, wood above

The houses keep their stone feet in the rain and live upstairs, behind sliding windows of capiz shell that turn lamplight soft.

04Simbahan, the churchᜐᜒᜋ᜔ᜊᜑᜈ᜔

The church at the top of the town

Its doors stay open late. Light from inside reaches halfway down the plaza, and the bell tower is the last thing you lose in the fog.

05Liwanag, the lightᜎᜒᜏᜈᜄ᜔

Then the parols come on

From above the plaza the whole town shows. Star lanterns light up string by string, all the way down the street to the water.

The craft

Five things the town is made of

Every part of the walk comes from a Filipino craft. The town is imagined; these are not.

  1. 01

    Capiz windows

    Panes cut from the flat shell of the windowpane oyster, set in a sliding timber frame. They let the light in and keep the glare out.

    Seen in Bahay na bato
  2. 02

    Bahay na bato

    A ground floor of stone or brick under an upper storey of wood: a house built to ride out earthquakes and floods.

    Seen in Bahay na bato
  3. 03

    The parol

    A star lantern of bamboo strips and paper, hung for a Christmas season that in the Philippines starts in September.

    Seen in Liwanag
  4. 04

    The bangka

    A narrow hull with bamboo outriggers on both sides, steady enough to fish from on open water.

    Seen in Lawa
  5. 05

    Votive candles

    Sold by the bundle outside church doors, and lit for a prayer or for someone missed.

    Seen in Hagdan
